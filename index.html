Rocket Racer Redesign

Studworks Toys sells the Rocket Racer, a toy car built from bricks. The factory builds every car by hand before it is boxed. You are the new product manager. Make the car cheaper to build without making it boring for kids.

The big idea: every extra piece costs worker time, and every different kind of piece needs its own mold, storage bin, and spot on the shelf. Fewer pieces and fewer kinds of pieces make a product cheaper to make.

True story: in the early 2000s, a famous toy-brick maker almost went bankrupt. One reason was that the number of different pieces it made had roughly doubled in a few years. Cutting them back was part of how it recovered.

How profit works: Studworks gets $12 for every car sold and expects to sell 50,000. Each piece costs about 6¢ of worker time, and each kind of piece costs 10¢ per car to store and handle. New molds cost money up front. And if the car gets less fun, fewer kids buy it.

Back on the factory floor. Build your redesigned car and see how much faster it goes. If you change your design in Step 2, build it again here.

What do these words mean?
Piece
One part a worker picks up and attaches. The original car has 46.
Kind of piece
A different shape or color. Two red 1×2 bricks are the same kind. A red 1×2 and a blue 1×2 are two kinds. Each kind needs its own mold, bin, and inventory count.
Mold
A steel tool that shapes plastic. A brand-new piece shape needs a new mold, paid for once before production.
Fun for kids
Customers buy brick toys to build them. If you glue too much together, the toy is cheaper to make but less fun, and fewer kids buy it.
Design for Manufacturing (DFM)
Designing a product from the start so it's easy and cheap to make, not just nice to use.
